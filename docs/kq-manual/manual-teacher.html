Your Sunday with KidzQuest

The same tablet your helpers use, plus one thing that's yours.

Thank you for teaching with KidzQuest. This covers the tablet at the door. Most of it is exactly what your helpers do, so it's worth a read even where it feels familiar, because they'll come to you with questions. The last part is the bit only you can do.

Getting going

1

Sign in and pick your room

Open the link Ate sends you, sign in, then tap your room.

You'll be asked to choose your own password the first time. Only the room you're teaching in will open.

Try signing in at home during the week. It's much easier than sorting it out on a Sunday morning.

If several of you are in the same room on different tablets, that's fine. You're all looking at the same register and you'll each see what the others tap.

3

Checking in and allergies

Find the child, tap Check in, tap the adult who brought them.

Two tags print with the same code. One goes on the child, the other goes to the adult and comes back at the end.

A red badge means an allergy, and it prints on the child's tag too.

Have a quick look down the register at the start and make sure everyone helping knows who has an allergy. It's easier than remembering mid-morning.

An empty allergy box only means nobody has told us yet. If a parent mentions something, pass it to Ate so it's there next week.

A child who isn't on the list

Bottom of the check-in list, tap Add a child who isn't on the list.

Name, the adult who brought them, a phone number, and allergies. Ate fills in the rest during the week.

Whoever is named here is the person who can collect that child today. Anyone else will need you to approve it, so it's worth a second look at the name and spelling.

Your helpers can do this too. If one of them looks unsure, it's a nice moment to stand next to them for the first one.

When someone new comes to collect

A helper will come and find you when someone arrives who isn't on a child's list. This one is yours. Two people looking is simply safer, and it makes a mix-up far less likely.

Nearly every time it's a grandmother, an auntie, a family friend or a driver, and everything is completely fine. The point isn't suspicion. It's just that somebody stops for thirty seconds and has a proper look.

Three things worth checking

1

Does the child know them?

Ask the child rather than the adult. A little one who lights up and reaches for someone tells you more than any piece of paper.

2

Can you reach a parent?

The child's contact numbers are on the screen. A thirty second phone call settles almost every one of these, and parents are usually glad you rang.

3

Does anything feel off?

Someone in an unusual hurry, unwilling to wait a moment, or a story that shifts while you're talking. You'll know it if you feel it, and it's worth paying attention to.

It's completely fine to ask someone to wait. "Let me give Mum a quick ring, I won't be a minute" is a perfectly normal thing to say, and nobody at HIF will think twice about it. Any small awkwardness is over in a minute. Being careful with a child is never something you'll regret.
6

Writing it down

Tap Someone else is picking up, then add their name and what happened.

Write it the way you'd explain it to someone next week. A few words is plenty, but "OK" doesn't help anybody later:

"Mum rang ahead at 10:15. This is Adam's auntie, Linh knows her, and she had the collection tag."

Your name and the time are saved with it. It can't be edited afterwards, which is exactly what makes it useful if anyone ever asks.

If you'd rather not

Then don't. Keep the child with you, stay friendly, and ring the parent. If you can't reach anyone, find Ate or one of the pastors.

A child spending an extra twenty minutes in a warm room with you is a very small thing. Everyone would far rather that than the alternative.

A tablet stops working

Switch to the paper register and write names down. If children had been checked in on that tablet while the dot was amber, assume those didn't save and rebuild the list from the room. Then let Ate know.

Someone made a mistake

Put it right and say so. Every change is kept with a note of who made it, which is the whole point. A record nobody can explain is a far bigger problem than a correction.

Please don't let anyone leave something wrong because fixing it feels like admitting to something. Ate would much rather hear about it.
